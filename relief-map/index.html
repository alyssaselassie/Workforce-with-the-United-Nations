    What the map suggests

    Suggestions from the team

    Pilot plan: one team or department

    1. Choose a team of about 15 to 40 people with a leader who will act on the results. Smaller teams can take part, but group lines will stay hidden.
    2. Check privacy first. Confirm the organization's data protection rules, use the anonymous link, turn on "Anonymize responses" and agree that groups under 5 are never reported.
    3. Explain it to the team: why the survey matters, that it is voluntary and anonymous, who sees what, and that no individual results go to supervisors.
    4. Open the survey for two weeks, with one reminder. Aim for at least 60% of the team.
    5. Share the map with everyone at once, then hold a one-hour team debrief: what surprises us, what we can change, and two or three actions with owners.
    6. Learn and repeat: ask participants about clarity, length and how safe they felt answering, test on phones and with a screen reader, and run the survey again in three to six months.

    How it works

    The survey (about 6 minutes, on any phone)

    For each of six workplace settings, people rate five statements from Strongly disagree (1) to Strongly agree (5), or choose Does not apply to me. Five optional questions about role, gender, age group, working language and disability allow group comparisons, and one open question asks for an idea to improve the team.

      The statements and their sources

        Scoring

        Each person's score for a setting is the average of the statements they answered (at least 3 of 5), turned around so that higher means more discomfort: discomfort = 6 − average agreement. The map shows the average across people.

        Privacy rules

        • No names, staff IDs, IP addresses or locations are collected, and every question is optional.
        • Nothing is shown for fewer than 5 answers, and no group smaller than the chosen minimum is ever shown.
        • People in small groups, and those who chose not to say, are pooled. If that pool is smaller than the minimum, the comparison is hidden, so no one can work out a small group's answers from the others.
        • Only one identity question is compared at a time, so small intersections are never shown.
        • Comments are never shown word for word: summarize them as themes.

        Use your own results

        1. In Qualtrics, export the responses as a CSV file with numeric values.
        2. Select Load a Qualtrics export above. The map is drawn in your browser, and the file is never uploaded.
