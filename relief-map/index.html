    The survey

    About 5 minutes on any phone. Every question is optional except consent, and nothing identifies the person answering.

    1. Introduction and consent. Purpose, voluntary and anonymous participation, how results are reported. Choosing "No" ends the survey.
    2. Workplace experience. Six settings, one short page each. Every setting has five statements, one per dimension, and each person sees three of them, chosen at random.
    3. Overall workplace experience. Five statements: belonging, support, confidence to raise concerns, respect and collaboration.
    4. About you (optional). One question asks whether to show five identity questions, each with "Prefer not to answer".
    5. Your ideas (optional). Two open questions, with a reminder not to include names.
    6. Thank you. A closing message about how results will be shared.

    Why each person sees 3 of the 5 statements

    A complete map needs one statement for each dimension in every setting: 30 in all. Showing each person 3 per setting keeps the survey to about 5 minutes. Qualtrics picks the three at random and evenly, so across the team every statement is answered by about 60% of people, and each point on the map still averages many answers.

    The trade-off: each point rests on fewer answers, so the map works best for teams of 25 or more, and group comparisons for a single dimension are hidden more often.

      Pilot plan: one team or department

      1. Choose a team of about 25 to 60 people whose leader will act on the results. Smaller teams can take part, but more points and group lines will stay hidden.
      2. Check privacy and ethics first. Confirm data protection rules and any ethics review, use only the anonymous link, turn on "Anonymize responses" and agree that groups under 5 are never reported.
      3. Test before launch. Five to eight volunteers take the survey on their phones and with a screen reader, then say what was unclear or felt too personal.
      4. Explain it to the team: why it matters, that it is voluntary and anonymous, who sees what, and that no individual results go to supervisors.
      5. Open the survey for two weeks with one reminder. Aim for at least 60% of the team.
      6. Share the map with everyone at once, then hold a one-hour debrief: what surprises us, what we can change, and two or three actions with owners.
      7. Learn and repeat: check completion time, skipped questions and how the statements hang together, adjust the wording, and run the survey again in three to six months.

      How it works

      Scoring

      • Answers are scored 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). Every statement is worded positively, so higher always means a more positive experience and nothing is reverse-scored.
      • Each point on the map is the average of everyone who answered that statement (one dimension in one setting).
      • A setting's score is first worked out for each person, as the average of the statements they answered there (at least 2 of the 3 they saw), then averaged across people.
      • A dimension's average is the average of its six points.
      • The overall index is each person's average of the five overall statements (at least 3 answered), averaged across people.
      • "% agree" is the share answering 4 or 5. As a guide: 4.0 or higher is strong, 3.0 to 3.9 is mixed, and below 3.0 means more disagreement than agreement.

      Missing answers

      • Statements a person was not shown (the random three, or the virtual setting after "No") are blank in the export and are left out.
      • Statements a person saw but skipped are exported as -99 and are also left out, never counted as 0 or as neutral. The statement table reports how often each was skipped.
      • Partial responses count: each number uses the answers that exist, and the number of answers is always shown.
      • Responses without consent, Qualtrics previews and test responses are removed.

      Privacy rules

      • No names, emails, staff IDs, IP addresses or locations are collected, and only consent is required.
      • No number is shown for fewer than 5 answers, and no group with fewer answers than the chosen minimum is ever shown.
      • People in small groups, and those who did not say, are pooled. If that pool is smaller than the minimum, the comparison is hidden, so no one can work out a small group's answers from the others.
      • Only one identity question is compared at a time, so small intersections are never shown.
      • Comments are never shown. They are summarized as themes, and a theme is reported only if at least 3 people raised it.

      Use your own results

      1. In Qualtrics, export the responses as a CSV with Use numeric values and Recode seen but unanswered questions as -99.
      2. Select Load a Qualtrics export above. The map is drawn in your browser, and the file is never uploaded.

      A workplace adaptation of Relief Maps by Maria Rodó-Zárate (Rodó-Zárate, 2023), building on the Relief Maps Tool developed in 2025 by the NYU Intersectionality Lab (Prof. Raúl Sánchez, Prof. Kristine Rodriguez-Kerr and Pietro Bonfante). The original method maps one person's emotional experience of places across their identities; this prototype keeps the link between identity, place and emotion but maps team averages, so it is not a reproduction of the original. Statements are adapted from Edmondson (1999), COPSOQ III (Burr et al., 2019) and Eisenberger et al. (1986), or newly written, as the implementation package details.
